Where does the BA sit in a project?

The BA is responsible for

Doing the right work (build the right thing): understanding the problem correctly, locking the right scope, and writing requirements clear enough for dev to build and for testers to verify.

The BA is not responsible for

Schedule and cost (the PM's job), how the code is written (the dev's job), the final business decision (the client's or the PO's job). The BA lays out options and analysis. The person with the authority makes the decision.

Warm-up · 15 min

5 foundational questions

  1. How is a BA different from a PM?
  2. The client says “I want an app”. Why shouldn't the BA start writing requirements for the app right away?
  3. What is the difference between a Functional requirement and a Non-functional requirement? Give one example of each.
  4. Why does the BA need to write meeting notes and send them the same day?
  5. When the client and dev disagree, whose side should the BA take?
View sample answer open after you have answered
  1. The BA owns “what to build”, the PM owns “how to deliver it on time and on budget”. The BA is responsible for requirements being correct and clear. The PM is responsible for schedule, cost, resources and risk. In a small company one person may play both roles, but the two responsibilities stay separate.
  2. An “app” is a solution the client came up with, not the problem. The BA needs to ask back up the chain: what is going wrong, how much money or time is it costing, who is affected. Sometimes the best solution is a website, a Google Form, or a process change with no software at all.
  3. An FR describes what the system does, e.g. “Customers can book an appointment with their phone number”. An NFR describes how well the system does it, e.g. “The booking page finishes loading within 3 seconds on 4G”. An NFR must be measurable.
  4. Meeting notes are evidence of what was agreed. After a few days, each side remembers things differently. Sending them the same day lets people correct them while memories are fresh, and means nobody can later say “I never agreed to that”.
  5. Neither side. The BA sides with the project's goals. The BA clarifies each side's reasons, lays out the options with the gains and losses of each, then lets the person with the authority decide. The BA does not decide on the client's behalf and does not force dev's hand either.

Identify the stakeholders

What you need to know

A stakeholder is anyone affected by the system or able to affect it. The people most often forgotten are the daily users. They don’t sign the contract, but they are the ones who decide whether the system is actually used or left to gather dust.

A common tool is the Power/Interest grid. It helps you decide how much attention each person needs:

Power ↑
Keep satisfiedHigh power, low interest. Short, well-timed updates.
Manage closelyHigh power, high interest. Meet often, ask for decisions.
MonitorLow power, low interest. Inform when needed.
Keep informedLow power, high interest. Ask for input, show demos.
Interest →

Specification · 90 min

Write User Stories and Acceptance Criteria

What you need to know

  • A User Story follows the template: As a [role], I want [action], so that [benefit]. The “so that” part explains the reason. When a situation comes up that the story does not cover, the dev looks at the reason to make a sensible decision.
  • Acceptance Criteria (AC) are the conditions for a story to count as done. AC are written as Given (context) · When (action) · Then (expected result). AC must be testable and include numbers where needed.
  • A good story meets the INVEST criteria: Independent, Negotiable, Valuable, Estimable, Small, Testable.

Your task

Self-check

  • Each story serves only one role and one goal
  • AC have numbers: time, character limits, quantities
  • There are AC for exceptions: an item just sold out, tapping submit twice, transferring the wrong amount

Trigger conditions (SR-TF-01, SR-TF-02): the transaction amount is greater than 10,000,000 VND, or the total successfully transferred during the day plus the current transaction is greater than 20,000,000 VND. How “the day” is calculated follows Legal's confirmation (Q-P-01).

Main flow:

  1. The customer enters the transfer details and taps Continue.
  2. The system checks the trigger conditions.
  3. The system shows the face capture screen with instructions.
  4. The system checks liveness, then matches against the stored biometric data.
  5. The match score meets the threshold set by Risk Management (a configuration parameter, not hard-coded). The customer moves on to OTP or Smart OTP verification.
  6. The transaction is sent, and the system writes a complete log.

Rules: BR-BIO-01, at most 3 failed authentications per transaction and 5 per day; beyond that, the transfer function is locked until the end of the day. BR-BIO-02, a customer with no stored biometric data must first update their biometrics (flow 4.2) before making a transaction that falls under the mandatory rule.

Exceptions: liveness failed; face does not match; virtual camera detected; 60-second timeout; connection lost midway. See appendix A for the message in each case.

Logged data: transaction ID, customer ID, device ID, timestamp, trigger reason, liveness score, match score, threshold in effect, result, vendor model version.

References: R5 → SR-TF-01, SR-TF-02, SR-TF-03 · NFR-04, NFR-06 · Abuse cases 2, 5.

Fundamentals

Questions 1–4

  1. What is a BA? In your view, what is the most important part of a BA's job?
  2. How do a User Story and a Use Case differ? When do you use which?
  3. Write me a user story and acceptance criteria for a login feature.
  4. How do Functional and Non-functional requirements differ? Give an example.
See suggested answers open after you have answered
  1. A BA is the bridge between business needs and the technical team. The most important part is understanding the problem correctly before discussing solutions. For example, at Mộc Coffee the client wanted to “build an app”, but the real problem was slow ordering and checking bank transfers by hand.
  2. A User Story is short, focused on value, and used for planning and conversation in Agile. A Use Case is more detailed, with a main flow, alternative flows and exceptions, and suits complex flows or when full documentation is needed. In practice the two are often used together: stories in the backlog, use cases to specify the hard flows, as in the Dental project.
  3. “As a user with an account, I want to log in with my phone number and password, so that I can access my information.” AC: correct login goes to the home page; a wrong password shows a generic error that does not say whether the phone number or the password was wrong; 5 failed attempts lock the account for 15 minutes; the password field masks characters; there is a forgot-password link. Bonus points: bringing up the security case and the account lockout case yourself, without the interviewer prompting you.
  4. FR is what the system does; NFR is how well it does it. Stress that an NFR must be measurable, and give an example such as “95% of page loads under 3 seconds on 4G”.

Scenarios

Questions 5–8

  1. The client wants to add a feature while the project is underway. What do you do?
  2. Two stakeholders ask for opposite things. How do you handle it?
  3. A developer says your requirement cannot be done. How do you respond?
  4. The client does not know what they want. Where do you start?
See suggested answers open after you have answered
  1. Log it, but do not promise anything yet. Clarify the request. Analyze the impact with the team. Present options with cost and time. The client decides. Update the documents and the backlog. If the scope has been signed off, go through the CR process. You can describe the loyalty points case at Mộc Coffee or the attendance allowance at Phú Thịnh Garment.
  2. Find the root reason behind each side. Propose an option that addresses both concerns. Escalate to the person with the authority to decide. Record it in the decision log. Describe the choose-your-dentist case at Nụ Cười Dental.
  3. Ask why: is it technically impossible, not feasible in the time, or too expensive? Go back to the business goal behind the requirement and look for another way to reach the same goal.
